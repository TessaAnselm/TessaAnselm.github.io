My Story

My path here started with a Linux instructor who noticed I was using file manipulation instead of loops on an assignment and encouraged me to look into cybersecurity. Around the same time, I accidentally accessed a database while exploring a CCSF server. I could not retrace how I got there, but I saw enough to realize how strangely systems behave when you poke at them the right way.

Before transitioning fully into cybersecurity, I spent years in client centered support roles where I learned how to communicate clearly, build trust, and remain calm in high pressure situations. That experience shaped how I approach technical leadership today by focusing not only on solving technical problems, but also on helping people navigate complexity with clarity and confidence.

While battling a serious illness during the pandemic with uncertain odds, I enrolled in school and discovered that learning was not just something I wanted. It became what kept me going. I came out the other side with a 4.0 GPA, Dean’s Honor List recognition, seven certifications, and a nationally recognized cybersecurity chapter I founded at CCSF that is still active today.

I am now pursuing a Master’s degree in Cybersecurity at the Georgia Institute of Technology while continuing to build projects focused on cybersecurity, cloud infrastructure, AI assisted development, and practical engineering.

More recently, after reviewing a project another cybersecurity learner had heavily built with AI, I became fascinated with AI assisted rapid prototyping and modern engineering workflows. While I admired the motivation behind the project, I also saw how easily AI built systems can become inefficient without strong programming foundations, structure, and engineering judgment. That experience pushed me to combine my programming background, frontend experience, cloud and security training, and AI tools to build projects more intentionally and effectively.
