My Story

My path into technology started unexpectedly. During a Linux course at City College of San Francisco, my instructor noticed I was solving a file manipulation assignment without using loops and encouraged me to explore cybersecurity. Around the same time, while experimenting with a CCSF server, I accidentally accessed part of a database. I couldn’t retrace exactly how I got there, but the experience sparked a curiosity about how complex systems behave, how they’re built, and why small changes can produce unexpected results.

Before transitioning into technology, I spent years in client-centered support roles where I learned how to communicate clearly, build trust, and stay calm under pressure. Those experiences continue to shape how I approach engineering today. I enjoy solving technical problems, but I also care about making complex ideas understandable and helping others navigate new technologies with confidence.

During the pandemic, while recovering from a serious illness with an uncertain prognosis, I enrolled in school. What began as a way to keep moving forward became a genuine passion for learning and building. I graduated with a 4.0 GPA, earned multiple industry certifications, founded the Women in Cybersecurity chapter at City College of San Francisco, and continue to lead and support technical communities.

Today, I am pursuing a Master’s degree in Cybersecurity at the Georgia Institute of Technology while building applications that combine AI, cloud infrastructure, APIs, and security engineering.

More recently, after reviewing an AI-generated project built by another cybersecurity learner, I became fascinated by AI-assisted software development. I was impressed by how quickly modern AI tools could transform an idea into a working prototype, but I was equally interested in the engineering work required to turn that prototype into reliable software. That curiosity has led me to explore agentic workflows, modern developer tooling, and AI-assisted engineering while applying the security mindset I’ve developed throughout my career. I enjoy building practical applications that are not only functional, but also maintainable, secure, and designed with the end user in mind.
